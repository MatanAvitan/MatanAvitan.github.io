About

I’m a PhD candidate in the NLP Lab at Bar-Ilan University, advised by Prof. Yoav Goldberg and Dr. Yanai Elazar. My research combines mechanistic interpretability, representation geometry, and causal interventions. I’m drawn to problems where a mathematical explanation of a model’s behavior makes a prediction I can test by changing its activations.

I also work at Cato Networks. Previously, I was an AI Research Scientist at IBM Research, where I developed algorithms for generating text over encrypted data. My background also includes building production ML systems for search analytics at Similarweb and malware detection at Cynet Security.

Publications

  1. NeurIPS 2026First author

    MANCE: Manifold-Aware Concept Erasure

    Matan Avitan, Yoav Goldberg, Yanai Elazar

    Can we erase a concept from a model’s representation while preserving other information encoded alongside it? MANCE estimates the local geometry of naturally occurring activations and uses it to constrain each edit. Across 119 settings in language and vision, these constraints improved the trade-off between removing the target concept and preserving other attributes.

  2. NeurIPS 2026First author

    Position Without Positional Embeddings: A Directional Mechanism in NoPE Transformers

    Matan Avitan, Ido Nachum, Yanai Elazar, Yoav Goldberg

    How does a Transformer know whether a token is the fifth or the fiftieth without positional embeddings? We identify a two-block circuit that uses the beginning-of-sequence token as a shared reference. Attention to that token falls predictably as the prefix grows, approximately as k/(k + i). The circuit writes that signal into an activation direction that survives LayerNorm. We find the mechanism in trained models, including a language model trained on next-token prediction.

    • NeurIPS page ↗

  3. AISTATS 2026

    PowerSoftmax: Towards Secure LLM Inference Over Encrypted Data

    Itamar Zimerman, Allon Adir, Ehud Aharoni, Matan Avitan, Moran Baruch, Nir Drucker, Jenny Lerner, Ramy Masalha, Reut Moshe, Omri Soceanu

    We develop a self-attention mechanism that polynomials can approximate efficiently, which makes homomorphic inference practical. We use it to train language models with over a billion parameters. We presented an earlier version of this work at FHE.org 2025.

  4. Findings of ACL, IJCNLP-AACL 2025First author

    Efficient Decoding Methods for Language Models on Encrypted Data

    Matan Avitan, Moran Baruch, Nir Drucker, Itamar Zimerman, Yoav Goldberg

    We introduce CutMax, an argmax algorithm for greedy decoding under homomorphic encryption, and a compatible nucleus sampling method for stochastic decoding. On realistic language-model outputs, the methods achieved speedups of 24× to 35× over the baselines we tested.

  5. Findings of ACL, NAACL 2025First author

    A Practical Method for Generating String Counterfactuals

    Matan Avitan, Ryan Cotterell, Yoav Goldberg, Shauli Ravfogel

    When we edit a model’s representation of a biography, what changes in the text? We turn edited representations back into readable counterfactuals, making it possible to inspect how an intervention changes the wording. We also use the resulting examples as training data to reduce gender bias in occupation classification.

03

Research interests

  • i.

    Superposition and sparse feature learning

    How do models encode more features than they have activation dimensions? I’m interested in using sparse autoencoders and dictionary learning to study how features share directions and how an edit to one feature affects others.

  • ii.

    How training shapes learned mechanisms

    I’m interested in when a circuit emerges, how the objective and initialization affect it, and whether the model relies on it when making predictions.

  • iii.

    World models and learned dynamics

    Can we recover a simple physical rule, such as constant-velocity motion, directly from a model’s activations? I’m interested in what a change of basis or a symbolic readout can reveal.

  • iv.

    Embedding inversion and reinforcement learning

    I’ve been exploring how a learned value function could guide text generation toward a target embedding. The value function estimates which partial strings are likely to end in a good match.

  • v.

    Diversity in open-ended generation

    I’m interested in how to generate several legitimate answers to the same question and measure whether they differ in substance.
